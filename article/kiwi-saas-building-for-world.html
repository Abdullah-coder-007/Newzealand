Building for the world from a small country

New Zealand punches above its weight in software. What early-stage founders can learn from those who've scaled.

By Daniel Park9 October 20266 min read

New Zealand has produced software companies that reach the world from a home market of five million. For early founders, our size is a feature: it forces you to think globally and build lean from the start.

Niche first, world second

The founders who break out tend to solve one specific problem exceptionally well before they broaden. A small home market means you can test, learn and refine with real customers quickly, then take a proven product to much larger markets offshore.

Watch your runway, build a product customers would miss if it vanished, and get close enough to users that their problems shape what you build. Distance from the big markets is real — but a good product and the internet have never made it smaller.
